Session 1: Discovery — a big country show

Fact file: the Great Yorkshire Show

  • Where: the Great Yorkshire Showground, Harrogate, North Yorkshire, England.
  • When: four days every July. In 2025 it ran from Tuesday 8 to Friday 11 July. The next one is on 14 to 17 July 2026.
  • Visitors: 140,000 people in 2025. Tickets are sold in advance only, with a limit of 35,000 visitors a day, and they sold out.
  • Animals: about 8,500 animals were exhibited in 2025.
  • It was the 166th show in 2025, so it is one of the oldest agricultural shows in the country.

Source: Show Director's Report 2025, Yorkshire Agricultural Society.

Session 2: Reading — a day on the stall

The story of Emma and Dale Farm Dairy is fictional, set at a real show.

The gates of the Great Yorkshire Show open at 7.30 every morning, but Emma has been on the showground since six. She is nineteen and runs the stall of her family's business, Dale Farm Dairy, with her cousin Sam. The family makes four cheeses: a mild Wensleydale, a crumbly Dales cheddar, a creamy blue and a smoked cheese that has won a prize at a regional competition.

Before the first customers arrive, Emma checks the display. The cheeses sit on wooden boards, every product has a clear price tag, and a plate of free samples is ready at the front of the stall. "People buy with their eyes first," her mother always says, "and then with their mouths." Sam prepares the card reader, because most visitors now pay contactless.

By ten o'clock the food hall is crowded. Some visitors only taste and walk away; others ask questions: Is it made with raw milk? How long does it keep? Could it travel in a car all afternoon? A man who runs a restaurant asks for the strongest cheese on the stall, while a young couple are looking for a present that is cheaper than the large blue cheese. Emma listens, asks what they like, and suggests a gift box with three small cheeses and a jar of chutney for £18.

At four o'clock, a shop owner from York stops at the stall. He tastes the smoked cheese twice, asks how many kilos the farm can produce each week and whether Dale Farm could deliver every Friday. Before leaving, he asks for Emma's business card. "If his customers like it," Emma tells Sam, "we could be in his shop by September."

C. Read between the lines

Explain the mother's words: "People buy with their eyes first, and then with their mouths."

The display must look attractive to make people stop; then the taste convinces them to buy.

Why does Emma ask the couple what they like before she suggests a product?

To understand their needs, so that her advice is useful and they are more likely to buy.

Why is the shop owner's visit the most important moment of the day?

Because he could become a regular customer: the family would sell all year, not only at the show.

Session 3: Grammar: asking questions

A good seller answers questions, but also asks questions to discover what the customer needs.

Yes/No questionsIs it made with raw milk? · Do you take cards? · Can I try it? · Have you tasted the blue?
Wh- questionsWhat would you like? · How long does it keep? · Which one is the strongest? · How much is the gift box?
Polite requestsCould I help you? · Would you like a bag? · May I suggest something?
Indirect questionsCould you tell me how much this cheese costs? · Do you know if it is organic? (no "do/does", normal word order: subject + verb)
Question tagsIt's delicious, isn't it? · You've been here before, haven't you?

Session 4: Numbers, prices and comparing

Useful facts: In the UK, prices are in pounds sterling: £1 = 100 pence (100p). Food is sold in grams and kilos, but many people still talk in pounds (lb) and ounces (oz): 1 lb ≈ 454 g · 1 oz ≈ 28 g · 1 pint ≈ 568 ml.

A. Read the prices aloud

£6.50 (six pounds fifty) · £18 · 75p · £2.99 · £120

B. Calculate

Wensleydale costs £2.60 for 100 g. How much is 250 g?

£6.50.

A customer asks for "a pound of cheddar". About how many grams do you cut?

£6 change (3 × £18 = £54).

£12.
