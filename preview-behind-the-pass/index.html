Session 1: Discovery — the brigade

In a professional kitchen, every cook has a place in the brigade. The pass is the counter where the head chef checks every plate before a waiter takes it to the dining room.

Head chefruns the kitchen, creates the menu, checks plates at the pass
Sous-chefthe second in command, replaces the head chef
Section chef (chef de partie)is responsible for one section: starters, fish, meat or pastry
Commis chefa junior cook who helps a section chef
Kitchen porterwashes the equipment and keeps the kitchen clean

Why does a kitchen need a clear brigade?

So that everybody knows their job and the kitchen can work fast, safely and calmly during service.

Session 2: Reading — service at noon

Chef Morgan and the hotel school are fictional.

It is 10 a.m. in the training kitchen of the hotel school. Service starts at noon and the restaurant is fully booked: forty-two guests, including a table of eight business people and a couple celebrating their anniversary. Chef Morgan has divided the brigade into four sections: starters, fish, meat and pastry. Each cook knows exactly what to do, and the menu is written on the board above the pass.

Before cooking, everybody prepares the mise en place. Vegetables are peeled and chopped, stocks and sauces are reduced, and the fresh fish is checked as soon as it arrives from the supplier: clear eyes, red gills, no strong smell. "If your mise en place is not ready, your service will be a disaster," the chef often says. The kitchen is hot and noisy, but nobody shouts. When the chef calls an order, the cooks answer "Yes, Chef!" and work quickly and cleanly.

At 12.15, the first tickets arrive. One guest is allergic to nuts, another is vegetarian, and a third wants to know where the lamb comes from. Chef Morgan reminds the brigade that every allergy must be written on the ticket and that a separate board and clean utensils must be used. At the pass, he tastes the sauces and checks each plate: the temperature, the portion, the presentation. A plate that is not perfect goes back.

At the end of the service, a waiter comes into the kitchen with a message: the couple at table six would like to meet the cook who prepared the roast lamb. Today you were on the meat section. You take off your apron, check your jacket, and go to the dining room. You will have to explain the recipe in English.

C. Read between the lines

Why is the mise en place so important?

Because during service there is no time to prepare: without it, the cooks cannot work fast.

Why must the cooks use a separate board for the nut allergy?

To avoid cross-contamination: even a small trace of nuts can be dangerous.

What does the guests' request show about the lamb?

They loved it and want to congratulate the cook; it is a reward for good work.

Session 3: Grammar: asking questions

In a restaurant, guests ask questions and staff must ask and answer politely.

Yes/No questionsIs it spicy? · Does it contain nuts? · Can I have the recipe? · Have you cooked it before?
Wh- questionsWhat is in the sauce? · Where does the lamb come from? · How long does it take? · Which wine goes with it?
Polite requestsWould you like…? · May I take your order? · Could you wait a moment, please?
Indirect questionsCould you tell me where the lamb comes from? · I'd like to know if the soup is vegetarian. (no "do/does", normal word order: subject + verb)
Passive questionsHow is the lamb cooked? · What is the sauce made with?

Fact: food businesses in the UK must tell customers if a dish contains any of 14 major allergens, for example peanuts, nuts, milk, eggs, fish, crustaceans, celery, mustard, sesame and gluten. Source: Food Standards Agency: allergen guidance for food businesses.

B. Explain a recipe: imperatives and sequencers

Sequencers: first · then · next · after that · while · finally. Imperatives: preheat · season · sear · roast · rest · slice · serve.

Put the steps of the roast lamb in order.

rest the meat · preheat the oven · slice and serve · season the lamb · roast for 90 minutes · sear in a hot pan

First, preheat the oven to 180 °C. Then season the lamb. Next, sear it in a hot pan. After that, roast it for 90 minutes. Then let the meat rest for 15 minutes. Finally, slice and serve.

Session 5: Production — meet the guests

A. Role-play in pairs

Student A is a guest at table six. Student B is the cook who prepared the roast lamb. Then swap roles.

The guest: congratulate the cook and ask at least five questions, including one indirect question, one about the ingredients and one about the cooking time.
The cook: thank the guest, present the dish, explain the recipe step by step with sequencers, answer the questions, say goodbye politely.
